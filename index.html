04 / Who is Sneferu?Meidum and Dahshur, around 2600 BC

Three attempts.
One direction.

Around 2600 BC the pharaoh Sneferu set out to build something nobody had built: a true, smooth-sided pyramid. It took him three tries.

  1. 01
    Meidum. The first began as a stepped pyramid and was reshaped, late, into a smooth one. Its casing is gone; how it came off is still argued.
  2. 02
    The Bent Pyramid. The second started steep. Partway up, the ground gave way: 23 centimetres, measured, still visible. The builders plastered the cracks, changed the angle from about 54° to about 43°, kept going, and then walked away.
  3. 03
    The Red Pyramid. The third, built at the shallower angle from the ground up, worked: the first true smooth-sided pyramid brought to completion.

Sneferu kept building until he got it right. That's the company.

Ask us something ↗
Is Sneferu a model or a coding assistant?+

Neither. Sneferu is the system around the models: planning, execution, tools, memory, review, and recovery. Software is one kind of work it finishes. Businesses, research, and games run on their own production lines on the same system.

Why different models?+

Different model families have different blind spots. Sneferu gives them different responsibilities and lets independently trained lineages challenge the work. Agreement alone proves nothing. Tests, validators, real data, and runtime evidence can overrule every model in the room.

What does “finished” mean here?+

The outcome met the checks for that kind of work. A software build needs execution evidence; a research claim needs support the system tried to refute. A completed run is not automatically a qualified product, and Sneferu says so. Remaining problems stay on the record instead of disappearing.

What is Snef?+

Snef is the Sneferu Brain: the operator you talk to. It reads the state of the system, uses memory and tools, and turns a goal into a plan and the right kind of run, with permissions and launch controls intact.

Where can I see how it really works?+

Inside Sneferu walks through every named part in plain language. The System Atlas maps the architecture, relationships, and implementation.

How can I work with Sneferu?+

Software On Demand is live for application builds. For the wider system, research collaboration, or a partnership, talk to the founder about what you want to finish.

Built with Sneferu / Infrastructure

ESM.
Memory with a history.

Governed long-term memory for Sneferu, and for other AI applications. A memory service that keeps track of what an entry means, where it came from, and how it changed.

More than saved text

Facts, preferences, decisions, and warnings are typed entries with provenance and confidence. Related memories are retrieved as context for later work.

Change without amnesia

Conflict checks surface clashing claims. Supersession, review, and retirement let the system update what it remembers while preserving the trail.

Typed intake→Conflict checks→Governed storage→Recall

A separate memory service with a FastAPI interface and Postgres / pgvector storage. The diagram describes its architecture, not a live activity feed.

Built with Sneferu for Sneferu / Model improvement

FDC Studio.
Better has to be proven.

Sneferu built a workshop that studies a model’s failures, finds the training examples it needs next, and tests whether a new version deserves to replace the old one.

The recorded demo uses simulated model responses. It demonstrates the decision process and rejection of bad candidates, not a completed production fine-tune.

Open the full-size decision screen ↗

Focused improvement

Test the model. Group recurring mistakes. Gather traceable examples for those weaknesses. Mix in earlier examples to protect existing skills, train a candidate, then evaluate it on a separate test.

One kept. Two rejected.

The bundled demo runs three attempts against a fixed 120-item test. One improvement is accepted; two are rejected. It ends with 36 traceable training examples and the last good model intact.

Every decision leaves a record.

The audit ledger records 33 events, including the accepted improvement and both rejected attempts. The final candidate made tool use worse, so FDC kept the previous version.

The original request

“Invent a novel fine-tuning method for optimal LLM performance in a target domain.” Idea Bloom explored 156 approaches; three survived the argument. The chosen direction became FDC Studio through Sneferu’s specification, code, and test pipeline.

The design connects the model being improved, independent judges, search tools, human experts, and a training system. Training and the decision to promote a model stay separate.
